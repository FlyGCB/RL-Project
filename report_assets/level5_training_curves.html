Diagnosis — Feature-DQN 5B collapses mid-training. Win rate against its own training-time opponents climbs cleanly through the first third of 5B, peaking at 81% around episode 500, then erodes almost monotonically to 27% by episode 1400 before a small uptick at 1500. Reward tracks the same arc (peak ≈8.0 at ep. 500 → trough ≈2.7 at ep. 1400). Training stopped at episode 1500 — mid-decline, not at a stable endpoint — which is why the final 5B checkpoint loses to frozen L4 (24%) and even to its own 5A checkpoint (44% head-to-head), despite 5A alone beating frozen L4 54% of the time. This is the classic self-play non-stationarity failure the notebook's opponent pool was meant to dampen — the pool visibly slowed but did not prevent it (self-mirror match stayed a non-degenerate 20/80 split rather than collapsing to 0–0 draws, so the agent didn't go fully inert, it just kept chasing a moving target). The practical read: 1500 episodes landed in a bad phase of an oscillating dynamic, not at convergence.

Contrast — CNN-DQN 5B improves steadily, no collapse. Win rate dips through the noisy middle third (episodes 500–800, driven by opponent-pool snapshots getting harder as the pool fills) then climbs through the back half to 77% by episode 1500 — still rising when training stopped, no sign of the Feature-DQN pattern. The much larger CNN capacity (conv stack vs. a 12-dim feature MLP) likely gives it a more stable self-play trajectory: more room to represent a robust policy rather than overfitting to whichever opponent it saw most recently.
